## Must fix before the run

**M-1. The masked replay can extend the attended grating's dominance, so "cannot extend" is false and M1 does not isolate self-extension.** The code does what the plan says. `on = rec[:, idx] & A`: it is on only where the baseline record and current dominance both say grating 1 (see F-1). The masked replay is on during an attended period up to the baseline's switch time, and the boost it has given carries the dominance past that time. In the diagnostic it lengthened the attended grating by 9 to 21%, against 28 to 100% for the live gate. It also has less on-time inside current attended dominance (84 to 97%, against 100%), partly because of desynchronisation, which is not extension. So live minus masked measures the effect of the on-time the gate adds after the baseline's switch time, plus a dose difference. It does not measure the effect of extension as such. If M1 fails, that does not show extension is irrelevant. If M1 holds, it shows only that more extension gives more lengthening. Fix: in Section 1 (Q3) and Section 4, reword the claim to "cannot be on after the baseline's switch time, though the boost it has given can still extend dominance". Pre-specify that each condition's attended change (already computed) and on-fraction within current grating-1 dominance (add it to `run_chunk7`: `on[A].mean()`) are reported next to M1. Also report the ratio of competitor change to attended change, live against masked, so readers can tell "lengthening follows extension" apart from "the gate does something beyond extension".

**M-2. P1 is close to untestable as specified when *r*<sub>ab</sub> is near zero.** In the pilot, the accepted Li configurations had *r*<sub>ab</sub> from −0.20 to 0.10 (median −0.015), and 9 of 16 were negative. A 16-seed estimate will have a sampling SE of about 0.05 (from the sixth test's split halves) to about 0.09 (scaled to the longer durations here). The true spread among accepted configurations is smaller than that. Two problems follow. First, the ranking of the product *r*<sub>h</sub> × attended change is driven mainly by the noise in *r*<sub>h</sub> and its sign; it is not mostly a test of attended change alone. Second, for configurations with negative *r*<sub>h</sub>, a larger attended change gives a lower predicted rank, which reverses the relation the predictor is meant to capture. A rough Monte Carlo (`p1_power.py`; assumed true *r* ~ N(0.06, 0.04), competitor noise SE 3%) gave these rates of the lower bound exceeding 0: 0.77 if competitor change ∝ *r* × attended change, 0.34 if it ∝ attended change only, and 0.00 under the null. The attended change alone gave 1.00 in the second case. Fix: pre-specify how negative *r*<sub>h</sub> is handled. Either keep it signed and state the consequence, or use max(*r*<sub>h</sub>, 0), with ties ranked by average. State in Section 5 that, in this range, P1 asks whether *r*<sub>ab</sub> still carries information and that failure is the expected outcome if the lengthening tracks attended change alone. Add the attended-change-alone version of the pooled within-model test, with the same bootstrap, as a pre-specified secondary so that a P1 failure can be read.

**M-3. The analysis crashes on the shortfall path the plan promises to handle.** If a model accepts no configuration, `model_block` raises `KeyError: 'gated'`. If every model has 3 or fewer usable configurations, `pooled_within` raises `ValueError` (`np.concatenate([])`). Both were confirmed with `edge_zero.json` and `edge_three.json`. The plan says that if fewer than 60 are accepted, "the run continues with those, and the hypotheses are tested on them", but it gives no minimum. Fix: (a) in the analysis, skip a model with no configurations, and in `pooled_within` return NaN when no group qualifies. (b) In the plan, add a minimum, for example "a hypothesis is not tested in a model with fewer than 20 accepted configurations; P1 requires at least two models with at least 20". With n = 20 the Wilson rule needs 15 of 20 to hold.

**M-4. The plan describes verification that the folder does not yet show.** `code_sha256.txt` is cited in Section 2 but is missing from `li2017_test7/`. `dry7_tests.json` was written by an earlier version of `analyse_li2017_test7.py`: it contains `ratio_comp_to_pred` and `proportional_at_observer`, which the current script does not produce. The current script runs on `dry7.json` and returns all changes as 0 (`precheck/dry7_tests_rerun.json`). Fix: regenerate `dry7_tests.json` and `synth7_tests.json` with the committed analysis, write `code_sha256.txt` after the last edit, and commit them together.

## Should fix

**S-1. Selecting on *r*<sub>ab</sub> < 0.1 changes what G1 is about, and the plan should state that.** The acceptance runs use independent noise (see F-2), so the selection does not bias the main-run measurements. The estimand, though, is "configurations whose 8-seed estimate was below 0.1", not "configurations with true *r*<sub>ab</sub> < 0.1". With 8 seeds and long durations (Li configurations had 11 to 44 episodes per seed, so about 5 to 22 attended–competitor pairs; `serial_r` returns NaN below 5), the acceptance estimate has an SE of about 0.1. The noise therefore admits configurations whose true *r* is 0.1 to 0.2, and the long-duration configurations are over-represented because their estimates are the noisiest. The selection also picks a regime: low *r* comes from weak adaptation relative to noise. In the rate models the accepted pilot sets were bimodal. In dep, 13 of 16 had T < 1.3 s and CV 0.65 to 0.80, just above the 500 ms floor; a minority had T = 5 to 9 s and CV 0.45 to 0.6. A failure of G1 could therefore reflect noise-driven switching rather than low *r* as such. Fix: state the estimand. Report the distribution of T and CV for accepted configurations, and report G1 by T tercile or by cluster. Consider 16 acceptance seeds, which halves the misclassification at about 2× the acceptance cost.

**S-2. The CV bound of 0.8 is defensible only with a sensitivity analysis.** The pilot shows that the bound is needed for the yield: in the rate models, *r*<sub>ab</sub> and CV are negatively correlated among configurations that pass the other criteria (−0.58 sfa, −0.51 dep). It also shows the cost. Among accepted pilot configurations, CV > 0.7 occurred in 3 of 16 (Li), 8 of 16 (sfa) and 9 of 16 (dep). Those distributions are close to exponential, whereas observers' gamma-like distributions are usually reported with CVs of about 0.4 to 0.6. The fit to observers' serial correlation is thus partly bought at the cost of the fit to their duration distributions. Fix: pre-specify G1, G2 and M1 on the subset with CV ≤ 0.7, reported with its n and without a decision. Note in Section 3 that about half of the rate-model sample is expected above 0.7.

**S-3. G1 is a sign count whose failure is ambiguous.** In the sixth test, the per-configuration SE of the gated competitor change (merged), estimated from split halves, was about 1.5 to 2% (median). The longer durations here (Li median about 5 s, against 1.8 s) scale that to about 3%. The sixth test's lowest-*r* configurations changed by 0 to 8% (Li), so the expected proportion of positive changes could be 0.65 to 0.8. That is near the Wilson threshold (38 of 60 needed), and failure would not distinguish "no lengthening" from "lengthening smaller than per-configuration noise". Fix: pre-specify, as reported quantities, the median change with a bootstrap 95% CI over configurations, and per-configuration SEs from split halves. Optionally state the smallest effect for which G1 has 80% power.

**S-4. The reported "G1 where main-run *r*<sub>ab</sub> < 0.1" selects and measures on the same seeds.** `G1_main_r_below_0_1_reported` takes *r*<sub>ab</sub> and the change from all 32 seeds. The selection then shares baseline noise with the change's denominator; for example, seeds with a few unusually long competitor episodes have both lower *r* and a larger baseline mean. Fix: select on seeds 0 to 15 and compute the change on seeds 16 to 31, as P1 does.

**S-5. The calibration OLS is attenuated and uses the same seeds on both sides.** The product *r*<sub>ab</sub> × attended change is measured with large error (M-2), so the slope is biased toward 0. The prediction at (0.06, 29%) is therefore close to the intercept, that is, close to the mean competitor change, whatever the true relation. All seeds feed both the predictor and the outcome. Fix: say so in Section 5, or fit the line on the split-half quantities. Report the intercept as the main calibration number.

**S-6. The synthetic check does not exercise P1.** In `synth7_tests.json`, P1 has 4 configurations per model and a constant imposed competitor change, which gives ρ = −0.5 and CI [−1, 0.5]; recovery is not shown. Fix: add a synthetic copy with at least 20 configurations per model, with competitor change on seeds 16 to 31 set as a monotone function of *r*<sub>h</sub> × attended change on seeds 0 to 15 plus noise. Confirm that the lower bound exceeds 0, and confirm that it does not when the relation is shuffled.

**S-7. Rejections are not fully accounted for, and acceptance runs serially.** In the last acceptance batch, candidates that pass after the target is reached are counted in `n_drawn` but in no rejection reason. Accepted plus rejected will then not equal drawn, although the plan reports "numbers accepted and rejected by reason". Fix: add `rej['surplus']`. Separately, `accept7` runs in the main process while the 6 workers sit idle. Results would not change if batches were computed in parallel and consumed in order, because the draws come from one sequential RNG and each batch's noise depends only on its own tag, seeds and configurations. This is optional.

**S-8. The on-time in competitor dominance is uninformative for two conditions.** `dose_comp` in `run_chunk7` is 0 by construction for the live gate and the masked replay (on requires grating-1 dominance). The diagnostic confirms 0.000. Fix: say so in Section 4, and add the within-grating-1 on-fraction from M-1, which does tell the conditions apart.

## Fine as is

**F-1. `Schedule7` is correct.** For `replay_masked` it calls `R3.Schedule3.__call__` once per step, which updates the merged-episode tracker and both observers; the Schedule3 mode is `'none'`, so it returns zeros, and the return value is unused. For every other mode it defers to `Schedule6.__call__`, which also calls Schedule3 once. `rec[:, idx]` reads the baseline's grating-1 dominance at the same integration step, from the state at the start of that step, exactly as `Arec[:, idx]` was written. The recorded check against the baseline trajectory agrees at 0.9995 to 0.9998; the residual comes from the unrecorded odd steps. `_stats` counts agreement and steps correctly. In the dry run every condition, including the masked replay, reproduces the baseline exactly. CRN keeps the conditions largely synchronised with the baseline record (86 to 96% for the masked replay), so the masked replay is close to "self-replay minus its competitor-time part", as intended.

**F-2. The acceptance noise is independent of the main run.** `run_batch7` seeds from `seed_of(tag + '_accept', seeds 0–7, configurations)` in acceptance and `seed_of('test7_<model>', seeds 0–31, chunk)` in the main run, and the latency pools are keyed by tag as well. Selecting on acceptance-run *r*<sub>ab</sub> therefore uses no main-run noise. `MAX_DRAWS` = 3,000 is generous given the pilot yields (Li 16/60, sfa 16/60, dep 16/80, which match the plan's stated quarter and fifth). About 225 to 300 draws per model are expected.

**F-3. P1 has no shared-noise path between predictor and outcome.** *r*<sub>h</sub> and the attended change come from baseline and gated seeds 0 to 15, and the competitor change from seeds 16 to 31. CRN pairs each seed only with itself. `thr` is per instance, from that instance's baseline. The per-configuration quantities pooled over all seeds (`T`, `thr_abs`) do not enter `mBm` or `mA`, although `thr_abs` would enter a P1 computed under the absolute criterion. The common configuration is the signal. Using the unmerged `mA` keeps P1 comparable with the sixth test. Brief grating-1 intrusions, the main way unmerged and merged scoring could diverge, were about 0 per minute in the diagnostic. Reporting `mOA` alongside would cost nothing.

**F-4. The analysis functions are correct.** `change()` is identical to the sixth test's (mean of per-seed means over seeds finite in both, NaN if the baseline mean is ≤ 0). The Wilson bound is correct (z = 1.959964), and ties count as failures. `pooled_within` and its bootstrap (resampling within models, average ranks / (n + 1), percentile CI, NaN-safe) match the sixth test. `QI = 9` is the 50th percentile of `QS = range(5, 100, 5)`. `dist_stats` returns None below 20 durations and is skipped by `if b and c`. `med` and `prop` drop NaNs. The polyfit is guarded.

**F-5. Runtime and memory fit the machine.** Each worker simulates one chunk of 10 configurations × 32 seeds = 320 instances at once, for 6 simulations (baseline, baseline-record, 4 conditions) of 240,001 steps each. The recorded arrays per simulation are `rb` 320 × 2 × 120,001 float32 = 307 MB, `on` 154 MB, two observer records of 38 MB each (int8), and `Arec` 77 MB (bool, 320 × 240,001). The peak, during the baseline-record run while `rb0` is still held, is about 0.95 GB per worker, or about 6 GB for 6 workers. Measured per-step cost on this container at N = 320 was 250 µs for Li and 80 µs for sfa and dep, about 60 s and 19 s per 120 s simulation. Long mean durations do not change the step cost; they only reduce episode counts. The estimates below are for this container; the Windows machine may differ. Li takes about 6 min per chunk, with all 6 chunks in parallel, plus serial acceptance (80 instances per batch, about 41 s) of about 16 min at the pilot yield. sfa and dep take about 3 + 7 min each. The total is about 45 to 60 min. If yields collapse, the worst case is about 3.4 h for Li's acceptance (300 batches).
